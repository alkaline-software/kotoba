# Changelog

## v0.1.3

1. **Choose the microphone.** Pick the one dictation listens to in General, Input; it follows your Mac's input unless you pick one.
2. **Quiet microphones are heard.** A microphone on an audio interface, or a quiet one, is no longer taken at a fraction of its loudness, and soft last words are no longer cut off.
3. **Softer chimes.** The start and stop chimes are quieter.
4. **Fixes.** Words dictated with no place to go in Kotoba's own window go to the clipboard without a notice pushing the settings down.

## v0.1.2

1. **F keys alone.** F1 to F20 can be the shortcut on their own: press one in the shortcut field, or choose it under More. A note says when F1 to F12 on an Apple keyboard do what is printed on them instead.
2. **Kinds of key.** More opens a list of kinds of key, One Modifier, Two Modifiers and F Keys, each with its keys beside it.
3. **One Kotoba at a time.** Opening one quits any other, so the shortcut no longer works twice.

## v0.1.1

1. **Accessibility when you ask.** Kotoba no longer asks for Accessibility on its first launch; the system asks only when you press Allow in Settings, General.
2. **Fixes.** Closing the settings with ⌘W takes Kotoba out of the Dock, as the close button does.

## v0.1.0

1. **Dictation in any app.** Press the shortcut, speak, press it again, and the words are pasted where you type, your clipboard put back after; a pill at the top of the screen shows it listening.
2. **In the menu bar.** A click on its icon opens the settings, and a right click shows a menu of each section and Quit; General can swap the two clicks or hide the icon.
3. **On this Mac.** Speech is written down by a model you download once (Qwen3-ASR or Whisper), in any language and two mixed; nothing you say leaves the machine.
4. **Post-processing.** When on, a small local model removes hesitations, applies corrections and mends grammar, keeping to what you said.
5. **Customize.** Preferences in plain words for post-processing, Terms written exactly as you save them, and words to Avoid, never written.
6. **Any key.** The shortcut can be a key or a modifier tapped alone, such as Right ⌘ or Fn; Esc cancels while listening.
7. **History.** What you dictated is kept for as long as you choose.
8. **Your language.** The window, its menu and the microphone's question follow your Mac's language, or the one you pick at the top of General: English, Chinese (Simplified and Traditional), Japanese, Korean, French, German or Spanish.
9. **Permissions first.** Settings, General asks for the microphone and Accessibility before your first dictation, and can open Kotoba at login.
